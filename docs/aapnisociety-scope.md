# AapniSociety Scope and Concept Note

Date: 2026-06-17

## 1. What this is

AapniSociety is a trust, mobility, and bargaining layer for the Indian urban need economy.

It is not primarily:
- a society-management product
- a public social network
- a bookings marketplace
- a ratings marketplace
- a landlord/RWA control tool
- a brokerage engine

It exists because Indian cities are not just collections of consumers. They are layered labor ecologies where tenants, domestic workers, gig workers, informal workers, and care workers reproduce city life while navigating caste, class, gender, religion, and housing exclusion.

The product should help these people:
- find trusted help
- preserve local knowledge across churn
- build worker bargaining power
- surface the city as workers actually experience it
- support private, consent-based trust transfer across households and buildings

## 2. The problem

The core problem is not “how do I book a service?”

The core problem is:
- how does a household enter a new building without blind trust
- how does a worker carry reputation and demand across buildings
- how does a tenant avoid starting from zero in a new city
- how does a person live inside a caste-class enclave without becoming visible in the wrong way
- how do workers describe the city from below, instead of being described by it

Urban India has a trust deficit built into everyday life:
- rental churn is high
- service labor is informal and fragmented
- brokers and RWAs often act as gatekeepers
- public ratings can become exclusion tools
- private households are not neutral spaces; they are often sites of hierarchy and coercion

## 3. Historical frame

### 3.1 Postcolonial capitalism and the need economy

Kalyan Sanyal is the most useful conceptual anchor here.

His argument, simplified for product design, is that postcolonial capitalism does not simply absorb everyone into stable wage labor. It produces a large population that lives in a space of unmet accumulation and ongoing need. People still have to reproduce life, even when they are not neatly inside the formal capitalist core.

That space is not residual. It is structural.

For AapniSociety, that means:
- tenants, domestic workers, gig workers, and informal workers are not edge cases
- the product should be designed for bargaining, mobility, and survival, not elite convenience
- the app should help people move through the need economy with more leverage and less exposure

### 3.2 Caste, class, and enclosure

Indian cities are not blank market spaces. They are often caste-class enclaves with thick informal control over:
- housing access
- service access
- visibility
- respectability
- women’s mobility
- worker dignity

So “trust” can easily become a euphemism for exclusion.

This product must therefore be anti-caste in structure:
- no caste-coded sorting
- no RWA-visible social graph
- no public worker ranking that becomes exclusionary
- no default collection of sensitive identity attributes

### 3.3 Labor history and collective organization

The product should learn from the Indian labor movement rather than ignoring it.

Relevant precedents:
- **SEWA**: worker-centered membership, services, mutual support, and cooperative logic
- **IFAT**: app-based transport workers organizing around algorithmic control and welfare
- **AIGWU / GIPSWU / TGPWU**: gig-worker organizing around pay, safety, and social security
- **KITU / UNITE / AIITEU / IIDEA**: tech-worker organizing around layoffs, working hours, and opaque management
- **Bidi and informal-worker histories**: home-based, feminized labor, arrears, social security, and collective ownership
- **Kerala Dinesh**: worker ownership as an economic form

These are not just political references. They show the product conditions workers face:
- fragile labor law enforcement
- retaliation
- opacity
- identity-based exclusion
- need for portable reputation and bargaining power

## 4. Product thesis

AapniSociety should become a worker- and tenant-authored urban intelligence system.

That means:
- workers define the city through their own signals
- households get trusted introductions without public exposure
- building memory survives tenant churn
- workers carry references, rate floors, and safety information across buildings
- the coop or platform does not extract value by taking a cut of every labor interaction

## 5. What the app should foreground

### 5.1 Trust transfer

People should be able to ask:
- who can introduce me?
- who in this building already knows a good worker?
- who has experience with this locality?
- what should stay private?

### 5.2 Worker bargaining power

Workers should be able to:
- carry references
- set minimum rates
- compare local demand
- block abusive households
- build portable trust
- author the city through their own experience

### 5.3 City intelligence from below

Workers and tenants should be able to describe:
- route reliability
- night safety
- harassment patterns
- repeat-client zones
- fair-pay neighborhoods
- hostile institutions

This is not “scenic routes.” It is practical urban intelligence.

## 6. Conceptual product zones

### 6.1 Household trust zone

Used by tenants and households to:
- join a building
- request introductions
- preserve local service memory
- navigate private support needs with discretion

### 6.2 Worker mobility zone

Used by workers to:
- carry references
- set rate floors
- record preferences
- store city notes
- avoid abuse

### 6.3 City safety zone

Used by workers and tenants to:
- flag unsafe lanes
- note transit reliability
- report hostile institutional behavior
- distinguish firsthand experience from rumor

### 6.4 Cooperative governance zone

Used by the worker cooperative or support organization to:
- moderate abuse
- manage member rules
- review aggregated patterns
- support grievance escalation
- operate the software as infrastructure, not extraction

## 7. Cities and rollout logic

Ahmedabad is the anchor because it has:
- strong cooperative precedent
- strong informal-work history
- strong migrant and housing dynamics

Phase 2 cities:
- Bengaluru
- Mumbai
- Delhi NCR
- Pune

Phase 3 cities:
- Hyderabad
- Chennai
- Kolkata

These cities matter because they combine:
- churn
- labor density
- platform work
- housing pressure
- organized and unorganized labor histories

### 7.2 Store-signal summary

Current store signals point to three useful observations:

- **Mygate**: Android Play listing shows 4.4 stars and 81.4K reviews; Apple App Store shows 4.7 stars and 103K ratings. Its product copy strongly centers society ERP, visitor control, domestic help, billing, and RWA operations. Some user reviews complain about ad overload and surveillance-by-notification dynamics, which is directly relevant to your privacy design.
- **NoBrokerHood**: Android Play listing shows 4.8 stars and 1M+ downloads; Apple App Store shows 3.9 stars with 46 ratings. Its copy centers society security, visitor management, home services, and household help. The product also reveals a broad gatekeeping-and-community-management orientation.
- **Urban Company**: Android Play listing shows 4.8 stars and 1.98M reviews with 10M+ downloads. Its copy centers fast home services, beauty, repairs, and domestic help. It is the clearest benchmark for the “trusted service marketplace” side of the problem, but it is not a worker-coop design.

This means the market already has:
- trust/society software
- service-market software
- and a lot of user feedback about privacy, convenience, ad load, and household visibility

That feedback should be treated as research input, not marketing noise.

## 8. Design implications

1. The product must be privacy-first.
2. The product must be worker-authored.
3. The product must avoid elite aesthetics.
4. The product must support portability.
5. The product must support anti-caste and anti-abuse defaults.
6. The product must not become a marketplace tollbooth.
7. The product must represent the majority city, not the elite city.

## 9. What success looks like

The product is conceptually successful if:
- a tenant can settle into a building without blind trust
- a worker can move buildings without losing reputation
- workers can tell the app what the city is really like
- the app improves bargaining power, not just convenience
- the system can grow into a cooperative infrastructure layer
