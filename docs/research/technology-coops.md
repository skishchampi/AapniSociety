# Technology Cooperatives and Platform Cooperatives

Date: 2026-06-17
Status: Draft research note

Purpose: identify technology cooperatives, platform cooperatives, app-building worker cooperatives, cooperative digital infrastructure, and platform-enabled worker organizations that can inform AapniSociety.

This note is focused on technology and full-stack cooperative infrastructure. It does not replace the broader labor and need-economy analysis in [aapnisociety-scope.md](../aapnisociety-scope.md).

## 1. Core Finding

The most useful comparator is not a generic cooperative.

The relevant model is a cooperative technology stack:

- worker-owned or member-governed app
- worker-controlled data
- transparent pricing and fee rules
- democratic product roadmap
- member onboarding and education
- grievance and moderation infrastructure
- portable reputation and references
- payment or settlement rails
- cooperative-friendly hosting and support
- federation across local chapters

The strongest examples do not treat technology as a neutral tool. They treat software, hosting, data, payments, and governance as political infrastructure.

## 2. Europe

### CoopCycle

CoopCycle is the closest software pattern for AapniSociety. It is a federation of bicycle delivery cooperatives using shared logistics software, training, hosting, and democratic governance. Its public site describes the software as a complete tool for worker-owned delivery coops, including order management, route planning, courier management, invoicing, and storefront functionality. It also uses a "Coopyleft" license intended to keep the platform from being appropriated by extractive firms.

Sources:
- https://coopcycle.org/en/
- https://github.com/coopcycle/coopcycle-web

Lessons:
- shared software should be paired with a federation
- local cooperatives need configurable instances
- roadmap decisions should be made by members
- platform fees should be explicit and democratically governed

### CoTech

CoTech is a UK network of creative technology companies owned and controlled by workers. Its members cover product strategy, web apps, CMS/CRM, e-commerce, data integrations, learning platforms, AI, cloud hosting, design, accessibility, and cooperative development.

Source: https://www.cotech.coop/

Lessons:
- AapniSociety can use a federated technology-worker model
- app development, hosting, support, and organizational design should be connected
- the co-op should not depend on a single heroic developer

### Outlandish and SPACE4

Outlandish is a worker-owned digital agency, and SPACE4 is a cooperative tech ecosystem space. Their relevance is not only software delivery, but ecosystem-building: events, training, workspace, cooperative incubation, and aligned client pipelines.

Sources:
- https://outlandish.com/
- https://space4.tech/

### Equal Care Co-op

Equal Care Co-op is a UK care/support platform with multi-stakeholder governance. It is useful for AapniSociety because domestic and care work is relationship-heavy and safety-sensitive. The lesson is that home-service platforms need care circles, references, recruitment, consent, and money-flow transparency, not just booking UX.

Source: https://www.equalcare.coop/

### Igalia

Igalia is a worker-owned free-software consultancy with deep technical expertise in browsers and open web infrastructure. It matters because it proves that worker-owned tech does not have to mean low technical ambition.

Source: https://www.igalia.com/

### Open Food Network

Open Food Network is an open-source marketplace infrastructure for food producers, shops, and local food hubs. It is relevant because it demonstrates hub-based marketplace architecture: local groups can organize inventory, producers, and orders using shared infrastructure.

Source: https://openfoodnetwork.org.uk/

### Cooperative Hosting and Digital Sovereignty

Hostsharing and Webarchitects show that hosting, backup, support, and abuse-handling can be cooperative infrastructure, not only vendor services.

Sources:
- https://www.hostsharing.net/
- https://www.webarchitects.coop/

## 3. North America

### Up & Go

Up & Go is the strongest direct analogy for AapniSociety's domestic-service side. It is a worker-owned cleaning platform in New York. Its public site says professionals are peer-selected through reference checks, interviews, and trial cleanings, and that only 5 cents of each dollar goes to Up & Go.

Source: https://www.upandgo.coop/

Lessons:
- worker onboarding should be staged and relational
- quality control can be peer-based instead of customer-rating based
- platform fees should be simple enough for workers and customers to understand
- domestic work should not become an opaque reverse auction

### The Drivers Cooperative

The Drivers Cooperative is a New York driver-owned rideshare and paratransit cooperative. Its site says drivers steer the company, profits go back to drivers, and members make 8 to 10 percent more per trip than on Uber and Lyft.

Source: https://drivers.coop/

Lessons:
- start in a dense, regulated niche before trying to beat incumbents everywhere
- worker income claims must be visible and testable
- cooperative transport platforms still need serious capital, support, dispatch, and compliance operations

### Eva Coop

Eva Coop is a Quebec ride-hailing/delivery cooperative. It is useful as a regulatory lesson: platform coops in transport need legal authorization, city strategy, insurance, and compliance from the beginning.

Source: https://eva.coop/

### CoLab Cooperative

CoLab is a worker-owned web and app development cooperative that builds complex digital products for social-justice organizations. It is relevant as a possible model for building AapniSociety itself: a software shop can be worker-owned while delivering production-grade apps.

Source: https://colab.coop/about/

### Hypha Worker Co-operative

Hypha is a Toronto worker cooperative working on digital trust, decentralized infrastructure, verifiable media, open protocols, and public-interest technology. Its public handbook is particularly useful for onboarding, roles, compensation, governance, and data policies.

Sources:
- https://hypha.coop/
- https://handbook.hypha.coop/

### May First Movement Technology

May First is a democratic technology membership organization providing hosting, email, lists, Nextcloud, Discourse, Jitsi, and related infrastructure. It is relevant because AapniSociety will need trusted infrastructure for worker data, grievance logs, member documents, and communications.

Source: https://mayfirst.coop/

### Failure Archives: Ampled and Resonate

Ampled and Resonate are useful because they show that cooperative ownership does not by itself solve product-market fit, licensing, runway, support burden, or growth.

Sources:
- https://ampled.com/
- https://resonate.coop/

## 4. Latin America

### FACTTIC

FACTTIC is an Argentine federation of technology worker cooperatives. It is one of the best models for AapniSociety's long-term builder ecosystem: shared contracting, training, conflict mediation, social balance, public legitimacy, and inter-cooperation.

Source: https://facttic.org.ar/

### gcoop, Cambá, tecso

These Argentine technology cooperatives show different software-coop shapes:

- gcoop: free software, Drupal, ERP, infrastructure
- Cambá: software, creative technology, education
- tecso: larger enterprise software, fintech, security, agtech, AI/ML

Sources:
- https://www.gcoop.coop/
- https://facttic.org.ar/camba/
- https://tecso.coop/

### Sutty

Sutty is cooperative/autonomous free-software infrastructure for resilient publishing and static websites. It is relevant for security, low-resource websites, content warnings, accessibility, anti-censorship design, and source availability.

Source: https://sutty.nl/

### EITA

EITA is a Brazilian free-software cooperative that builds tools for social movements, education, collaboration, and agroecological commerce. It is a strong model for building software in dialogue with movements rather than as a vendor above them.

Source: https://eita.coop.br/

### Cataki

Cataki is not a worker cooperative, but it is an important platform-enabled informal-worker case. It connects waste generators with waste pickers and cooperatives. Its lesson is that informal-worker platforms must create recognition and income, not just collect data about workers.

Sources:
- https://www.cataki.org/
- https://pimpmycarroca.com/

### AppJusto

AppJusto was a Brazilian delivery platform that let couriers define work value and avoided punitive algorithmic management, but suspended operations. It is a key caution: ethical platform design needs capital, support, growth, and unit economics.

Source: https://appjusto.com.br/

## 5. Africa

Africa has fewer publicly documented formal worker-owned platform cooperatives. The strongest lessons come from cooperative digital infrastructure, SACCO fintech, open-source financial rails, and worker organizations using digital recognition systems.

### Zenzeleni

Zenzeleni is a community/cooperative-owned ISP in South Africa. It matters because it expands the meaning of "platform" beyond an app. Sometimes worker ownership requires owning the connectivity layer, support layer, or local infrastructure.

Source: https://zenzeleni.net/

### Cooperative Finance and Open Rails

Co-operative Bank of Kenya, Mifos, and Mojaloop point to the financial side of the stack: savings, credit, settlement, member ledgers, and payment interoperability.

Sources:
- https://www.co-opbank.co.ke/
- https://mifos.org/
- https://mojaloop.io/

### African Reclaimers Organisation

ARO is not a tech cooperative, but it is crucial for data governance. Digital IDs and service logs can help informal workers claim recognition and compensation, but only if workers control the terms.

Source: https://www.africanreclaimers.org/

### Cautionary Private Platforms

SweepSouth, SafeBoda, Afriwork, and Kwara are useful as operational references but not ownership models. They show onboarding, dispatch, verification, payments, low-bandwidth channels, safety buttons, and finance digitization. They also show the danger of platform-washing: worker-facing apps can remain investor-led or vendor-controlled.

Sources:
- https://sweepsouth.com/
- https://fair.work/en/ratings/platforms/south-africa-sweepsouth/
- https://safeboda.com/
- https://afriworket.com/

## 6. Asia and India

Asia has many powerful worker and informal-worker cooperatives, but fewer mature software/platform cooperatives in urban services.

The relevant Indian and Asian cases remain important for AapniSociety's full stack:

- SEWA Cooperative Federation for women informal-worker cooperative development: https://www.sewafederation.org/
- SWaCH Pune for worker-owned municipal-service integration: https://swachcoop.com/
- HomeNet South Asia for home-based worker organizing: https://www.homenetinternational.org/network/hnsa/
- HomeNet Southeast Asia for broader informal-worker networks including domestic workers, vendors, and transport workers: https://www.homenetinternational.org/network/hnsea/
- Japanese consumer and welfare coops for care-service scale and member infrastructure: https://jccu.coop/eng/

The lesson is that AapniSociety should not begin as "an app for workers." It should begin as a worker organization that uses an app as one part of member infrastructure.

## 7. Bharat Taxi Case Note

Bharat Taxi must be tracked, but treated cautiously.

Public reporting describes Bharat Taxi as a cooperative-led ride-hailing platform under Sahakar Taxi Cooperative Limited, launched with government support, with claims of zero or reduced commission, Rs 500 driver participation, profit sharing, and expansion from Delhi-NCR/Gujarat to other regions.

Sources:
- https://bharattaxiapp.com/
- Times of India, launch and expansion reporting: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/bharat-taxi-launched-in-india-expansion-across-country-in-3-years-all-details-here/articleshow/127935122.cms
- Times of India, Mumbai cooperative taxi controversy: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/mumbai/bjp-mlc-announces-devabhau-chhava-taxis-for-mumbai-damania-cong-flag-bank-fund-use/articleshow/131777067.cms
- Economic Times reporting on Rs 500 participation and profit-share claims: https://m.economictimes.com/news/new-updates/pay-rs-500-become-co-owner-bharat-taxis-investment-model-like-amul-that-became-a-rs-1-25l-cr-giant-from-rs-50-contributions/articleshow/128709886.cms

The risk is state-led cooperative branding without deep worker control.

For AapniSociety, Bharat Taxi is not a model unless these questions are answered:

1. Do drivers directly elect the board?
2. Can drivers vote on commission, pricing, penalties, app rules, and data use?
3. Who owns the software, dispatch algorithms, user data, and brand?
4. Can driver members inspect accounts and platform metrics?
5. Can drivers reject trips without algorithmic punishment?
6. Who disciplines drivers, and what appeal process exists?
7. Who controls expansion, credit, vehicle loans, and political partnerships?
8. What happens if the platform loses money?
9. Can drivers exit with their data, reputation, and share value?
10. Are women drivers and marginalized-caste/religion drivers structurally protected, or only rhetorically included?

Bharat Taxi should be listed as a watch case and cautionary comparator: cooperative language is insufficient if worker members do not control governance, data, pricing, discipline, and surplus.

## 8. Design Implications for AapniSociety

### 8.1 The Product Must Include Governance Surfaces

The PWA should eventually include:

- member status
- proposals
- votes
- meeting minutes
- policy changes
- fee changes
- board/operator disclosures
- grievance status
- audit logs visible to the relevant member class

Governance cannot live only in PDFs, WhatsApp groups, or founder memory.

### 8.2 Rate Floors Must Be Product Logic

The app should enforce:

- worker-defined rate floors
- cooperative floor rates by service category
- travel compensation
- cancellation policy
- emergency surcharge rules
- no reverse auctions
- no hidden ranking based on willingness to accept low pay

### 8.3 Data Must Be Cooperatively Governed

The system should define:

- individual worker data rights
- collective data rights
- export and deletion policies
- access logs
- member-readable data policies
- rules for sharing aggregate data with city/state/NGO partners
- rules for safety notes and hostile-household reports

### 8.4 Use Open and Portable Infrastructure

AapniSociety should avoid vendor capture by preferring:

- open-source core systems where practical
- portable database design
- clean API boundaries
- self-hostable deployment
- documented backups and restore
- co-op friendly hosting
- future integration with cooperative finance, SACCO, credit union, or public payment rails

### 8.5 Build Organization Before Marketplace Scale

The strongest examples show that software amplifies an existing organization. It does not create worker power by itself.

For `0.1.0`, this means:

- start with one worker cohort
- onboard manually
- run member education
- create cooperative rules before automated matching
- keep mediation human
- make the app a record and coordination layer first

## 9. Practical Comparator Set

For product design, use these as the primary comparator set:

- Up & Go: domestic-service marketplace with worker ownership
- CoopCycle: federated platform software and coopyleft licensing
- The Drivers Cooperative: ride-hailing worker cooperative with app economics
- FACTTIC: federation of technology worker cooperatives
- CoTech: technology-worker cooperative network
- Hypha: public handbook and worker-owned digital-trust practice
- May First: cooperative hosting and movement infrastructure
- EITA/Sutty: movement-aligned software and low-surveillance infrastructure
- Zenzeleni: community-owned connectivity
- Bharat Taxi: Indian watch case for state-led cooperative platform risk

## 10. Open Research Questions

1. Which Indian technology cooperatives or worker-owned software shops already exist?
2. Can AapniSociety partner with SEWA, SWaCH, or a domestic-worker union without being NGO-captured?
3. What Indian legal form best protects worker control while allowing software development, grants, and public contracts?
4. Can the app use a coopyleft or source-available license that prevents extraction while allowing adoption?
5. What financial rails should be used for member dues, service fees, and future payouts?
6. Should the technology team itself be worker-owned, or should the domestic/support-worker cooperative own the product and contract aligned tech coops?
7. What governance surfaces are required in `0.1.0`, and what can wait until `0.2.0`?
